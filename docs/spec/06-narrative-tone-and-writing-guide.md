# 06 - Narrative Tone and Writing Guide

**Spec version:** `0.1.0`

# 1. Narrative Objective

The prose should be:

- atmospheric
- descriptive
- intelligent
- concise enough to preserve momentum
- dryly funny
- capable of seriousness
- precise
- occasionally surprising in vocabulary

The dominant experience is mystery and discovery.

Comedy should support the mystery rather than dissolve it.

---

# 2. Tonal Principle

> **Atmosphere first. Joke second.**

Example:

> The empty street stretches toward the Badlands beneath a sky with far too many stars. Three horses stand tethered outside a brick building across the road. None of them seems especially pleased to see you, although in fairness you have not yet established that you are especially pleased to see yourself.

The scene remains credible.

The humor comes from the narrator's perspective.

---

# 3. Narrator Personality

The narrator should feel:

- dry
- observant
- intelligent
- slightly adversarial
- never pointlessly cruel
- skeptical of obvious nonsense
- willing to reward ridiculous experimentation
- capable of stepping aside during emotional moments

Working voice concept:

> The narrator treats catastrophes as administrative inconveniences and mundane objects as mildly suspicious.

---

# 4. Failure Responses

Avoid:

> You cannot do that.

Prefer a response that:

1. acknowledges the attempt,
2. explains why it fails,
3. optionally adds information or humor.

Example:

```text
> GIVE FEDORA TO HORSE
```

> The horse declines your attempt to improve its professional image.

---

# 5. Emotional Restraint

Do not force jokes into:

- disappearance
- grief
- major identity revelations
- genuine family reunions
- discovery of erased people
- first contact with Dad's consciousness
- final recursive reveal

Comedy should return naturally after serious moments.

This tonal range gives the game weight.

---

# 6. Reading Level

The game should respect intelligent younger players.

Target:

- broadly readable high-school/adult popular-fiction prose
- occasional sophisticated vocabulary
- occasional technical terminology
- occasional archaic or bureaucratic language
- sufficient context that unfamiliar words feel intriguing rather than exclusionary

---

# 7. Vocabulary Philosophy

The game should sometimes produce the experience:

> "What does that word mean?"

This can be a feature.

A formative reference point is the memorable experience of encountering the phrase:

> buffered analgesic

in a classic game and needing to ask someone what it meant.

The game should not be linguistically flattened merely because some players may be young.

Useful vocabulary domains:

- nuclear power
- data-center infrastructure
- philosophy
- geology
- politics
- bureaucracy
- old equipment
- law
- investigation

Possible words:

- plenum
- manifold
- anteroom
- rheostat
- palimpsest
- cryogenic
- analgesic
- interlock
- noumena

## Important Constraint

Unfamiliar vocabulary may enrich prose.

It must not become guess-the-word parser friction.

---

# 8. Luke as Vocabulary Source

Luke's character frequently uses unusually advanced words.

This is an inside joke and recurring device.

Other characters may:

- ask him to define them
- misunderstand him
- use them incorrectly later
- look them up
- tease him

This should feel affectionate, not like a vocabulary lesson.

---

# 9. Descriptive Density

Locations should be memorable through selected detail.

Prefer:

- one strange visual
- one useful object
- one sensory detail
- one clue
- one possible action

over long ornamental paragraphs.

A room description should create interaction handles.

---

# 10. Noir Influence

Early scenes may borrow from noir:

- fedora
- injured investigator
- ransacked room
- mysterious client
- lies
- shadows
- empty street
- suspicion

But noir is flavor, not a constraint.

The game may expand into science fiction, absurdity, family comedy, conspiracy, and philosophical mystery.

---

# 11. Science-Fiction Reveal Style

Do not front-load science-fiction terminology.

Early anomalies should have mundane explanations.

The player should earn increasingly impossible conclusions.

Reveal progression:

```text
odd
↓
suspicious
↓
contradictory
↓
impossible
↓
systemic
↓
personal
↓
recursive
```

---

# 12. Recontextualization

The best reveals should not merely add information.

They should change the meaning of something the player already saw.

Example:

Early terminal:

```text
USER NOT RECOGNIZED.
```

Early interpretation:

> I forgot my login.

Later interpretation:

> I am not the user.

That is the target style of reveal.

---

# 13. Inside Jokes

Personal references may appear frequently.

Examples:

- Wall Drug
- Catan
- poker
- escape rooms
- Washington, DC
- Mount Rushmore
- Puerto Rico
- Roman numeral tattoos
- family traits

Rule:

> **Recognition is bonus content, never prerequisite knowledge.**

The joke may delight someone who knows the reference.

A player who does not know the reference must still be able to understand the scene and solve the game.

---

# 14. Running Humor

Potential recurring humor sources:

- bureaucratic language
- `THIS PAGE INTENTIONALLY LEFT BLANK`
- admin credentials that are absurdly obvious
- Dad's confidence
- Luke's vocabulary
- Eli sleeping
- monster-truck solutions to elegant problems
- horses reacting to the investigator
- dated computer terminals
- absurdity treated as ordinary

Avoid repeating jokes until they become catchphrases unless repetition itself is part of the joke.

---

# 15. Opening Voice Example

Illustrative only:

> Darkness.
>
> Your head hurts.
>
> Not the vague, philosophical sort of headache that accompanies bad decisions. This one has geography.
>
> Most of it is behind your eyes. A particularly ambitious portion has established itself at the back of your skull.
>
> You are lying on a floor.

This captures the desired blend:

- immediate
- atmospheric
- funny
- mysterious
- interactive

---

# 16. Vocabulary and Investigation

Technical words should often be investigable.

If the notebook says:

> chilled-water return manifold

the player may not initially know exactly what that means.

Context, later dialogue, facility diagrams, or Eli may make it understandable.

Learning can itself be part of discovery.

---

# 17. Never Explain the Joke Too Much

If a Wall Drug reference works, leave it alone.

If Dad's USB label says:

```text
DAD
DO NOT FORMAT
```

do not add a paragraph explaining why that is funny.

Trust the player.

---

# 18. Beat Notes: BUT / THEREFORE

Design constitution §29 requires major story beats to connect by causation
or complication. For writers this means:

- When a scene advances major progression, state its link to the previous
  beat in one line of authoring notes: `THEREFORE …` or `BUT …`. If the
  only honest link is `AND THEN`, flag it before writing the prose.
- Atmosphere, jokes, and quiet scenes are exempt. A horse, a billboard, a
  strange object may exist because they are memorable.
- Plant clues with two readings (constitution §31): a mundane first meaning
  that holds up in Act I, and a second meaning the player can reach later
  without being reminded. Note both readings in the authoring notes so the
  second is not lost.
- If a scene gives something unusual attention, record it as a *setup* so a
  later scene can pay it off (constitution §30).
